Source and method (Nov 19, 2026): training/harth_thigh_check.py on the public HARTH dataset
(UCI, CC BY 4.0, 22 people, Axivity AX3 accelerometer on the right front thigh, 50/100 Hz).
Resampled to 40 Hz, 1.5 s windows, random forest 10 trees depth 6 balanced, leave one person
out, same as the wrist model. HARTH has no gyroscope: 7 of the thigh unit's 10 motion features
(no gyro_mean/std/max) + tilt mean/spread from the accelerometer only, relative to each
person's mean standing direction (stands in for the thigh unit's calibration).
Sedentary = standing, sitting, lying; shuffling, stairs and cycling left out.

Reading the result:
* A thigh accelerometer alone classifies the three activities across 22 people with a mean
  error of 2.9 % (3.0 % with tilt), similar to the wrist on PAMAP2 (3.3 %). Different people,
  sensor and dataset, so this is NOT a wrist-vs-thigh-vs-fused comparison (C9 needs both
  sensors on the same people at the same time).
* Tilt separates standing (median 7.6 deg) from sitting (84.1 deg) and lying (90.2 deg), as
  assumed (A3). Both are "sedentary" in this project, so tilt mainly reduces sedentary ->
  walking errors (945 -> 655 windows).
* Tilt lowered the error for 18 of 22 people but raised it for S029 (18.2 -> 35.2 %): S029's
  walking is unusually vigorous (acceleration spread 1.11 g, tilt 41 deg, like other people's
  running), so it is read as running. One outlier person dominates the mean.
