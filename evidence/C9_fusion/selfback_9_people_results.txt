Source and method (Nov 19, 2026): training/selfback_fusion_check.py on the public selfBACK
dataset (UCI, CC BY 4.0): Axivity AX3 accelerometers on the dominant wrist and the thigh,
worn TOGETHER, 100 Hz. First 9 people by ID (026-036), to match the 9 PAMAP2 people of the
wrist model. Resampled to 40 Hz, 1.5 s windows, random forest 10 trees depth 6 balanced, leave
one person out; every model uses the same windows and test person.
No gyroscope in selfBACK: accelerometer features only (wrist 7, thigh 7 + tilt mean/spread
from the person's standing direction = the thigh unit's calibration).

Reading the result (9 people):
* Wrist only 5.0 % mean error; thigh only 3.2 %; one fused model on both 4.5 %; late fusion
  (wrist and thigh models trained separately, votes averaged) 2.9 %.
* Late fusion had a lower error than wrist only for all 9 people. The single fused model beat
  wrist only for 6 of 9 but did worse than thigh alone on average: with 16 features and only
  8 training people per fold it fits person-specific details (with all 33 people it matches
  thigh alone: 1.3 %).
* Two people (026, 030) dominate every mean; with 9 people the differences between thigh,
  fused and late fusion are small compared with the person-to-person spread.
* Check with all 33 people (selfback_33_people_check.txt): wrist 2.8 %, thigh 1.3 %, fused
  1.3 %, late fusion 1.2 %; fusion beat wrist only for 31 of 33 people.
Limits: public data, Axivity sensors (not the watch or the MPU-6050), accelerometer only, so
this answers the fusion question for these sensors; the thigh unit's own recordings
(train_fusion.py) are still needed for the project's hardware.
